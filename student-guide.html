Student guide

How does an AI know that a puppy is like a dog, or that a photo of a tiger goes with the word “tiger”? It turns everything into lists of numbers. This app lets you look inside, and find things out that nobody has noticed yet.

The one big idea

The AI in this app turns a word, a picture, a sound or a video into a list of 768 numbers. That list is called an embedding. Things with similar meanings get similar lists. That's all the AI knows about them: it never “sees” the picture the way you do, it just compares lists of numbers.

Two lists are compared with a number called similarity. 1 means they point the same way, 0 means unrelated. Most of what you'll do is guess a similarity, then check.

You're the investigator

The AI in this app is a real one, and nobody, not even the people who made it, can fully explain everything it does. Why does it think “house” is close to “dog”? Why can't it tell “The dog bit the man” from “The man bit the dog”? If you find a pattern and test it, you're doing real research.

So bring your own ideas. Try the things you care about: your favorite games, foods, teams or animals, words in a language you speak at home, a photo of your pet, a recording of an instrument you play. Your own things are more interesting than ours, and more likely to surprise you.

Getting started

  1. Open the app in Chrome or Edge on a computer. Press Load in section 1. The first time, this can take a minute; after that it's quick.
  2. A few things are added for you: dog, cat, apple, house, a photo of a corgi, a photo of a house and a dog barking. Add your own with the box in section 2, or add a whole set with one button.
  3. Words with a dotted line under them explain themselves if you point at them or tap them.
  4. Stuck? Press 🧭 Guide in the corner to ask the AI guide. It can be wrong, so check what it says against the page.

How investigators work

  • Guess first. Before you press anything, write down what you expect. Being wrong is the interesting part: it means you've found something.
  • Keep a notebook. For each try: your question, your guess, what you did, what happened, and what surprised you.
  • Treat surprises like bugs. Don't just say “the AI got it wrong”. Ask why, and change one thing to find out.
  • Check it another way. Does your finding still hold with 768 numbers instead of 256? With “Take away what everything shares” ticked? With different words that mean the same?
  • Share it. Show your class what you found, with the numbers as evidence. Then let them try to break it, and try to break theirs.

Starting points

These missions are places to start, not a list to finish. Do them in any order, change them, skip the ones you don't like, or use one as a jumping-off point for an investigation of your own. Each ends with a Go further idea with no right answer.

Mission 1 · Who is close to whom?

Look at section 4. Which pair do you think scores highest: dog and cat, dog and apple, or dog and the photo of the corgi? Write your order down, then check.

Go further: make a set of your own things that you think the AI will group differently from the way you would. Were you right?

A hint

Words tend to score higher with other words, even when a picture is a better match. That's called the modality gap. So compare which picture is nearest a word, not just the size of the numbers.

Mission 2 · Why do all the stars look the same?

In section 3, each number is drawn as a ray: green for positive, red for negative. Look at the stars for dog and apple. They look almost the same! Now tick Take away what everything shares at the top. What changes?

Go further: with the shared part taken away, can you find two things that point in opposite directions, with a similarity below zero?

What's going on

About two thirds of every list is a part that all of them share. Taking it away leaves what makes each thing different. Then unrelated things score near 0.

Mission 3 · What does a ray mean?

Point at one of the longest rays of a star. The page shows everyday words that are unusually high or low for that number. Can you find a ray that seems to be about music, or sports? Can you find one that makes no sense at all?

Go further: pick a topic you care about and hunt for a ray that leans toward it. Give it a name, and test your name with new words.

The surprise

Most numbers don't mean one thing. The model mixes many ideas into each number. Ideas like “more than one” (dog → dogs) are spread over lots of numbers. Press one → many above the star to see it.

Mission 4 · Word math

In section 6, try puppy - dog + cat. What do you think it lands on? Then try (morning + night) / 2 and Paris - France + Japan.

Go further: before you look at the answer, can you predict a word math result using only the numbers in the grid?

Important

The answer can only be something that's on the page. The example buttons add a few possible answers for you, some right and some wrong. To test your own guess, use Add my guess. Or tick Also search 2,838 everyday words under the list, and the AI looks through a big list of words too (they're marked 📖). Was your guess on top?

How does word math work?

puppy - dog + cat is an analogy written as math: “puppy is to dog as what is to cat?” There are two ways to think about it.

Arrows. puppy − dog is an arrow from dog to puppy, roughly meaning “younger”. Follow the same arrow from cat and you land near kitten. It works because the arrow from cat to kitten points much the same way as the arrow from dog to puppy (not exactly, but far more than chance).

Adding up scores. The winner is whatever is most like puppy and cat, and least like dog. Check it in the grid: kitten's similarity to puppy, minus its similarity to dog, plus its similarity to cat, beats “pet”, which loses points for being so doggy. In section 5, tick Show how each score is made to see these sums.

Neither means the AI knows a kitten is a baby cat. It learned from how words are used.

Mission 5 · Invent a formula

Make up a formula of your own that you think will work, like king - man + woman or 2 * big - small. Write down what you expect, then try it. Can you find one that fails in a funny way?

Go further: start a formula zoo: ones that work, ones that fail, and ones that fail in funny ways, each with your best explanation of why.

Mission 6 · Pictures and sounds

Add the 🐾 Animals set. Which word is nearest the sound of a dog barking? Is the AI right? Then add a photo of your own (or drop one onto the page) and see which word it lands nearest.

Go further: find a picture or sound that the AI gets badly wrong. What might have confused it?

A known weak spot

In testing, the dog barking sound came out a little closer to “cat” than to “dog”. Speech and music work much better. Can you find out why? Nobody knows for sure.

Mission 7 · Build your own space

In section 7, choose three directions, like small → big, quiet → loud and sad → happy, and press Place everything. Where does the tiger land? Drag to turn the space around.

Go further: invent three directions that sort things you care about, like scary → cute or boring → exciting. Do your friends agree with where things landed?

Mission 8 · Test a famous claim

Geoffrey Hinton, one of the pioneers of modern AI, said that in word embeddings “man” is closer to “dog” than to “cat”, and “woman” closer to “cat” than to “dog”. Add the 🐕 Dogs, cats, men and women set. Look at the grid first: can you see it? Predict which word of each pair leans further toward cat, then press Check in the challenge box.

Go further: find another claim about AI, from the news, a video, or the AI guide, and design an experiment to test it.

Hint

Tiny differences can still matter if they all point the same way. How many pairs agree? Where could a model pick up a habit like this?

Mission 9 · Then and now

Add the ⏳ Then and now set. Each sentence comes with one that means the same and a look-alike. An older AI model from 2018 picked the look-alike every time. Predict which ones this model gets right, then press Check in the challenge box.

Go further: build a trap gallery of sentences that fool this model too, and challenge a friend to explain them.

Hint

Try swapping who does what (“The cat chased the mouse”), or an idiom (“He kicked the bucket”).

Mission 10 · Catch the guide out

Ask the 🧭 Guide to explain something, then check it against the numbers on the page. Did it get anything wrong? AI guides can sound sure of themselves even when they're mistaken.

Go further: write a better explanation than the guide gave, one that a younger student could understand.

Your own investigation

The best questions are the ones you come up with yourself. Here are some to get you thinking:

  • Does the AI understand my language? Do my words land next to their English translations?
  • Which of my favorite games, songs or teams does it think are alike, and why?
  • Can I find a word that's close to everything? Or one that's close to nothing?
  • Does a photo of my pet land nearest “cat”, “dog”, or something odd?
  • Can I make a sentence that means the opposite of another but scores almost the same?

Pick one, guess, test, check it another way, and share what you found. If you're not sure how to test an idea, the 🧭 Guide can suggest an experiment.

Words to know

Embedding
The list of numbers the AI makes for something. Here, 768 numbers long.
Similarity
How much two lists point the same way: 1 is the same, 0 is unrelated.
Ray
One line of a star. Each ray shows one of the numbers.
Analogy
A pattern like “puppy is to dog as kitten is to cat”. Word math tries to find the missing word.
Direction
What you get by subtracting one embedding from another, like big minus small. It points from one meaning toward the other.
Modality gap
Words stay closer to words, and pictures to pictures, even when they mean the same thing.
